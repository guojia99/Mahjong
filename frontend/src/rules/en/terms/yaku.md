# Yaku (Hand Patterns)

**Category**: Core Rules

Yaku is one of the mandatory conditions for winning in Japanese Mahjong. A winning hand must possess at least 1 Han of yaku.

## Yaku Classification

### 1 Han Yaku

The most basic yaku, such as Riichi, Tanyao, Yakuhai, Tsumo, etc.

### 2 Han Yaku

Medium-value yaku, such as Chiitoitsu, Sanshoku Doujun, Ikkitsuukan, Honchantaiyao, etc.

### 3 Han Yaku

Such as Hon'iisou (open), Junchantaiyao.

### 6 Han Yaku

Such as Chin'iisou.

### Yakuman (13 Han)

The highest yaku rank, such as Tenhou, Daisangen, Kokushi Musou, etc.

## Yaku Stacking

Multiple yaku can be achieved simultaneously and their han values stack:

- Example: Riichi (1 Han) + Tanyao (1 Han) + Tsumo (1 Han) = **3 Han**
- Example: Riichi (1 Han) + Pinfu (1 Han) + Tanyao (1 Han) + Ippatsu (1 Han) + 2 Dora = **4 Han + 2 Dora Han**

## Menzen Only vs. Open Hand Allowed

| Category | Description | Examples |
|----------|-------------|----------|
| Menzen Only | Must be in menzen state to qualify | Riichi, Pinfu, Iipekou, Chiitoitsu |
| Open Hand Allowed | Qualifies with or without melds | Tanyao, Yakuhai, Sanshoku Doujun |

Open-hand-allowed yaku typically have higher han values when achieved in menzen state (e.g., Hon'iisou: open 3 Han → menzen 6 Han).

## Composite Yakuman

When two or more yakuman are achieved simultaneously:

- Double yakuman → **Double Yakuman** (16 Han equivalent)
- Triple yakuman → **Triple Yakuman** (24 Han equivalent)
